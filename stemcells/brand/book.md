# The Stemcells brand book

What Stemcells is for, how it thinks, how it talks, how it looks and how it
moves. Read it before writing a word a person will read or drawing a thing a
person will see: the app, the phone, the site, the readme, a release note.

The mark and its files are in [README.md](README.md). The page that acts
this book out is `stemcells/site/`.

## In one line

**Stemcells makes it fun and effective to work with your agents.**

Both words have a job. _Effective_: you read less, decide faster, and never
wonder where anything stands. _Fun_: the agents are creatures you like
having around, and the one you talk to has a point of view. Fun never costs
effectiveness; where the two meet, effective wins.

## Why it exists

**The mission: the shortest way from a thought to the thing.**

You type a sentence and an agent makes the thing it describes. Writing the
code is no longer the slow part. What runs out is you: your attention, the
tabs you keep checking, the walls of text you skim for the one line that
matters.

So Stemcells takes the shape of your work and gets out of the way. Every
step, wait, click and paragraph between the thought and the thing is ours to
remove. When a choice is unclear, pick the one with less in between.

The core line says it: **Words become things.** It is true of the product
(a sentence becomes a feature), of the art (Aski is made of characters, and
what it throws becomes a thing) and of the name (a stem cell becomes whatever
is asked of it).

## Who it is for

People who run several coding agents at once and want to hold all of it in
their head without trying. Developers first. The words stay plain enough for
anyone who works with agents.

|                  |                                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What it is       | An open-source app for the coding agents you already use, on your Mac and your phone                |
| What it replaces | A terminal tab per agent, a chat list you keep re-reading, a question buried in a transcript        |
| Why it wins      | Every ask has an arc you read at a glance; answers come as pictures; the tool bends to how you work |

## The five ideas

Everything Stemcells says comes back to one of these. Each is a thing the
product does, so each can be shown.

### 1. Thrown until it lands

An ask is a throw. You send it, it flies toward its goal, and it lands or it
falls short. Falling short is not failing: it comes down where you are with
one clear question, and your answer throws it again. You throw until it
sticks the landing.

```
                     . · ˙ ˙ · .
                 . ˙      Flying   ˙ .
              . ˙                      ˙ .
   your     ˙                             ˙        the
   words  █                  Input         █      thing
  ─────────────────────────── ? ───────────────────────
           throw      falls short, asks,      Landed
                        flies again
```

It is one picture for every state an agent can be in, and it tells you what
to do in each: nothing while it flies, answer when it asks, read when it
lands.

### 2. Settled

Two questions cover every Cell: _is it in the air?_ and _is it off my mind?_

|                   | Still on your mind              | Off your mind                 |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **In the air**    | Flying                          | (it comes back when it lands) |
| **On the ground** | Input: it asks. Landed: read it | Settled: read, done, put away |

Landing is the agent's finish. Settling is yours. A settled Cell is put
away, not deleted: write to it and it flies again.

The four words a person reads, everywhere, in this order:

| Stage       | Means                                 | You                 |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| **Input**   | It fell short and waits for your word | Answer; it flies    |
| **Flying**  | In the air, on its way                | Nothing. Hands free |
| **Landed**  | It stuck the landing                  | Read it             |
| **Settled** | Put away by you                       | Nothing, ever again |

### 3. Shown, not told

Agents answer in text, and a lot of it. People read pictures faster. So
Stemcells teaches every agent to pick the form a person reads fastest, and
draws it:

| What it has to say  | The form                                     |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Numbers to compare  | A chart                                      |
| A flow or structure | A diagram                                    |
| A choice to make    | One question, with a preview of each option  |
| Where it stands     | One closing line: what is done, what is left |

This rule binds us too. Anything Stemcells publishes shows the idea before
it explains it. If a paragraph can be a picture, it is one.

### 4. It becomes what you need

A stem cell is plain until it is asked for something; then it becomes that.
So does a Cell: the same model becomes whatever the work in front of it asks
for, and grows the longer it flies alone. So does the app: one chat or
twelve, tabs or panes, the Mac or the phone, any agent you already pay for.

Stemcells never asks you to work its way. It takes the shape of yours.

### 5. One crew, on every machine

Your agents run on whatever you own: the laptop, the phone, the tablet, the
Macs in the closet. Stemcells makes them one room. Throw from the device in
your hand and it flies on the machine with the power. When one asks, every
screen knows, and you answer from whichever is closest.

Every screen says the same thing, because there is one crew, not one per
machine.

## How we talk

**Short, plain, dry.** A sentence a tired person understands the first
time. The wit is in what we notice, not in decoration.

- **Show, then say.** The picture carries the idea; the words name it.
- **Concrete nouns, active verbs.** _Idea, thing, word, throw, fly, land,
  settle, read, answer._
- **Real numbers.** Minutes, files and counts as they are, never rounded up.
- **One joke per moment, never on a decision.** An ask, a permission, an
  error you must act on and lost work get plain words.
- **Big feelings about small things, with a straight face.** No wink, no
  emoji, no exclamation mark.
- **Kind.** We tease the code and ourselves, never the person.

| Instead of                                          | Write                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| An AI-powered agentic orchestration platform        | A fun, effective way to work with your agents |
| Seamlessly supercharge your workflow                | Throw it. It lands.                           |
| The agent is currently working on your request      | Flying                                        |
| The agent requires your input to proceed            | Input                                         |
| Task completed successfully!                        | Landed                                        |
| Archive conversation                                | Settle                                        |
| Here is a detailed summary of the changes I made... | Done: tests pass, pull request open           |

Words we don't use: _AI-powered, agentic, seamless, frictionless,
supercharge, unleash, empower, orchestrate, workflow, revolutionary_. They
claim; we show.

The arc has a small vocabulary and keeps it: _throw, fly, land, fall short,
settle_. No runways, turbulence or takeoff. No lab coats either: the name is
a metaphor for becoming, never for medicine.

## How it looks

**Words are characters. The app is itself.** That is the core line, drawn.

- **Characters** are for what is still an idea: Aski, words in flight, a
  trail through the air. They sit on a grid of cells, one wide to two tall,
  and density is life: `. : - = + * # % @ █`. Lighter is looser and can still
  become anything.
- **The app is shown as itself.** Wherever the product appears, it is a real
  screen of the app in its window's frame, never a drawing of one. A camera
  moves over it and a spotlight says where to look. If the app changes, the
  screens are taken again (`stemcells/site/shots/`).
- **Cells are pixels,** from the app's own painter, in a colour and a look of
  their own. They stand on things: a window's top edge is their ground.
- **One turns into the other.** Characters travel and gather, and the thing
  is there where they land. A real screen is never decorated with characters,
  and an idea is never boxed in chrome.

The rest holds everywhere:

- **Night and ink.** Night `#0B0B0C`, ink `#EDE9E0`, a raised surface
  `#151514`. On paper: `#F4F1EA` and `#141414`.
- **Two voices of type.** What a person reads is set in the app's own type,
  the system's sans, tight and plain. One monospace is for the mark
  (`stemcells` with a block cursor), small labels, code, and anything made of
  characters.
- **Colour means something.** Each Cell wears its own. A stage wears the dot
  the app gives it: Input amber `#FDBE48`, Flying, Landed and Settled as the
  app's list shows them. Amber means Input and nothing else. Aski, the brand
  and every line of type stay in ink.
- **Nothing for show.** No gradients, no glow, no shadows, no coloured edge
  stripes. A border is one hairline of ink. Depth is said by size and by
  how faint a thing is.

## How it moves

Motion explains; it never decorates. A move that teaches nothing is cut.

1. **A real signal starts every move,** and the move lasts as long as the
   real thing does.
2. **Things turn into things.** A body comes apart into its characters, the
   characters travel, and they become the next body. Never a cut.
3. **One mover at a time,** and stillness between moves.
4. **Arcs come from gravity,** squash keeps volume, the eyes lead.
5. **Every frame is a function of time alone.** So a scene can be scrubbed,
   stopped, shown as a still and tested.
6. **Smooth or nothing.** Eased in and out. No flicker, no jolt.
7. **It stops out of sight,** and Reduce Motion gets the key still with its
   line.
